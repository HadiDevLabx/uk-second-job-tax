# Second job tax

Why a second job is taxed at `BR` with no allowance, when that is correct,
when you are overpaying, and how to split your personal allowance across two
employers.

**Live:** <https://uk-second-job-tax.pages.dev/>

One static page. No framework, no dependencies, no tracking, no build step.

## The expensive case is the one that looks fine

If two jobs together push you over £50,270, `BR` deducts **20%** on the second
job where **40%** is owed. Neither payslip looks wrong, and the shortfall
surfaces a year later as an underpayment collected through your tax code.

## The one case where two jobs beat one

National Insurance is calculated **per job, per pay period**, and is never
reconciled across employments the way income tax is. £9,000 + £9,000 from two
employers pays far less NI than £18,000 from one, because neither crosses the
threshold alone. Income tax treats the two cases identically.

## When BR is wrong

| Situation | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Main job over £12,570 | Correct — allowance fully used |
| Main job under £12,570 | Overpaying — ask HMRC to split the allowance |
| Combined over £50,270 | Underpaying — needs `D0`, not `BR` |
| Job 1 has ended | Wrong — send the P45 |
